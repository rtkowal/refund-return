# Consumer Complaint Database | Consumer Financial Protection Bureau

## About the database

### What is published and when

Only complaints sent to companies for response are eligible to be published and are only published after the company responds, confirming a commercial relationship or after 15 days, whichever comes first. The database generally updates daily. We do not publish complaints that the Bureau refers to other regulators, such as complaints about depository institutions with less than $10 billion in assets.

This database is not a statistical sample of consumers’ experiences in the marketplace, therefore these complaints are not representative of all consumers’ experiences with a financial product or company. Complaints are not “information” for purposes of the [Information Quality Act](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/open-government/information-quality-guidelines/).

*   [Learn more about the data we publish](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/complaint/data-use/)

### Understanding complaint volume

When looking at complaint volume about a company or product, consider company size and market share. For example, companies with more customers may have more complaints than companies with fewer customers. When looking at complaint volume for a state or ZIP code, consider the size of the population in that geographic area.

*   [See past products and issue updates](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/data-research/consumer-complaints/#past-product-and-issue-updates)

## How we use complaint data

Complaints can give us insights into problems people are experiencing in the marketplace and help us better regulate consumer financial products and services, enforce those laws judiciously, and educate and empower consumers to make more informed financial decisions.

*   [Learn how we use complaint data](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/complaint/data-use/)
*   [Read our latest annual report to Congress](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/data-research/research-reports/2025-consumer-response-annual-report/)
